# Inside a Mission

Missions are where the rest of Starforged Ascendant comes together.

Your Character build, Drone setup, Talents, equipment and combat choices all meet the objective waiting inside the Mission.

The current Mission setup includes **Power Core Capture**.

The goal is easy to understand:

reach the Power Core, secure the area and hold the objective while enemies push into the fight.

The interesting part is how your build handles that situation.

- A heavy frontline Character might stay close to the objective and absorb pressure as enemies arrive.
- A Rifle build can move toward the edge of the capture area and look for clean firing lanes.

- Gravity effects can pull enemies away from the Core.
- Knockdowns can buy a few seconds of breathing room.
- Movement Skills can quickly carry the Character from one side of the fight to the other.

The Drone can be healing, attacking, controlling space or supporting the Character through Merge while all of this is happening.

## Generated spaces

Mission generation creates the space around the objective.

Rooms, encounter areas and enemy placement shape how the fight develops. That means a familiar objective can still produce different combat situations. 
A Power Core placed in a wide room gives ranged Characters plenty of room to work. A tighter approach can make area control, melee and defensive Skills much more valuable.
Beyond the shape of the encounter, different outcomes may also occur, instead of fighting waves of enemies a boss may appear, or you may need to extract from the base before the timer runs out. 
Enemy routes also matter because the Character may need to deal with several directions at once while remaining close enough to keep control of the objective.

These objective changes allows different builds to thrive in different situations, while also allowing access to critical base resources.

## Primary and Secondary Objectives

The Primary Objective defines the main purpose of the Mission.

Each Mission Item currently has four Secondary Objective slots. Players can craft up to two Secondary Objectives onto the Mission Item, letting them target particular objectives, resources or equipment rewards.

Those additional tasks provide another reason to explore the generated space and take on extra risk.

Mission rewards and progression are connected to how the run plays out, so optional objectives can become part of the decision about how far you want to push the Mission.

## The complete combat loop

Missions are where buildcraft stops being theory.

A Skill that looked good on the loadout screen has to work while enemies are surrounding the objective.

A Drone configuration has to contribute when the Character is under pressure.

A defensive build has to hold the line.

A damage build has to clear enemies quickly enough.

A support build has to keep the group moving.

The Mission gives all of those systems a job to do.

Build the Character.

Configure the Drone.

Enter the Mission.

Then see how the whole setup performs when the objective starts fighting back.

## Infinite scaling

Mission Items can keep pushing beyond the difficulty a Character has already cleared.

Successfully completing a Mission at a given level can open access to a higher challenge, and crafting can also push a Mission Item to an exceptionally high Mission Level.

As the Mission Level climbs, enemies become significantly harder and the room for mistakes gets smaller. Builds that were comfortable several levels earlier may suddenly need stronger damage, better control, more reliable recovery or a different balance of defensive layers.
The reward scales with that risk.

Higher-level Mission Items can produce increasingly valuable rewards, giving developed endgame builds a reason to keep testing how far they can go.

Mission crafting also lets players influence the challenge in other ways, including the enemies, objectives and reward opportunities attached to the Mission Item.
That creates a simple endgame loop:
- build something stronger → push a harder Mission → earn rewards worthy of that challenge → push again.
- There is no fixed point where the Mission system has to stop asking more from the build.

If the Character can handle the next level, there is another challenge waiting.
